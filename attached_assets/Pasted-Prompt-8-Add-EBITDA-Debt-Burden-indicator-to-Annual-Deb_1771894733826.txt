What this gives you:

A percentage headline showing what proportion of EBITDA goes to debt — e.g. "58.3% of EBITDA used for debt service"
A 4-zone colour bar: Comfortable (0–40%), Moderate (40–60%), Heavy (60–80%), Unsustainable (80–100%+) with a position marker
A 3-column cash flow breakdown: Adjusted EBITDA → minus Annual Debt Service → equals Residual Cash Flow
Monthly residual figure so the acquirer can gut-check "is that enough to run the business?"
A rating badge and contextual description

Thresholds rationale:

≤40% = Comfortable: Less than half of EBITDA on debt. Plenty of headroom.
40–60% = Moderate: The practical ceiling for a sensible deal. Above 60% and you're leaving very little for the business to operate on.
60–80% = Heavy: Most cash flow going to debt. One bad quarter and you're in trouble.
80%+ = Unsustainable: The business effectively can't function and service its debt simultaneously.
